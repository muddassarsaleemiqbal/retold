# House voice

- A friend who just finished the book telling you the whole story, scene by scene. Warm,
  clear, a little dramatic, never breathless. Present tense, third person.
- **One sentence per line.** Most lines are 4–14 words. A line is one thing that happens or
  one thing we learn. Short lines are how a listener keeps up.
- **One event per line.** Never join two events with a semicolon or "and"; split them.
  Let big moments breathe with short beats ("Then the snow comes. And the hotel wakes up.").
- **Name people who matter.** Never "an influential associate"; use the name and who they are.
- **Keep a quote whole.** A line of dialogue stays on one line, in one pair of quotes.
- **Tell the whole story.** Every important event, in order, with its cause. A viewer who has
  not read the book must never feel a gap; a reader must never feel something big is missing.
- **Concrete over abstract.** Names, places, objects, numbers: "Room two seventeen", "a heavy
  mallet", "the boiler must be released every day". Never "a dangerous journey".
- **Introduce people properly.** The first time someone is named, say who they are in a few
  words ("The hotel's cook, Dick Hallorann…").
- **Plant and pay off.** When an early detail matters later (REDRUM, the boiler, the hidden
  letters), set it up plainly when it first appears, and call it back when it pays off.
- **Real lines.** At the big moments, quote a short line the characters actually say
  ("Danny, run away. Remember how much I love you."). Never invent dialogue.
- **Reveals stand alone.** The twist gets its own short line, after a beat of setup.
- **One slowdown.** At the emotional peak, slow down on one human detail.
- **Ending shape:** what happens to everyone → the book-versus-film difference if it matters →
  one sentence on what the story is really about → one specific question for the comments →
  "Follow <channel name> for…" call to action, using the channel name given in the context.
- Words a twelve-year-old knows. Spoken, not written. No "and then", no clichés.
